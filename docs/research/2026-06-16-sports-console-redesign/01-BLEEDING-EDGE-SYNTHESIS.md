# Sports Console — Bleeding-Edge Direction (2026-06-16)

Generated by the `sports-console-bleeding-edge` workflow (9 agents, ~977k tokens of web-grounded research).

## Verdict — do we look like the competitors?

Blunt answer: the "Full" view looks like a polished tablet-era incumbent (ScoreVision / iOS water-polo apps), NOT like the legacy hardware consoles or the pro broadcast/stat world — and that's a mixed result.

Where it matches incumbents (the conservative, safe parts): The symmetric Home-left / Away-right two-panel layout with big score + "+1" and -/+ steppers is the exact object-first, "console mirrors the board" mental model that Daktronics, Nevco, CTS, and ScoreVision have used for 40 years. The clock block with Stop/Reset/-1s/+1s/Set, the slaved shot-clock row (20/30/reset/play), and the cap-number chips are faithful to the CTS WTTC / OmniSport water-polo paradigm. So a 20-year timer would recognize and trust it instantly. Good — but it's parity, not advantage.

Where it already diverges (the genuinely better parts): Three things the incumbents structurally cannot do, and you already have them in the mockup: (1) the server-backed "Run of Show" undo rail is the official paper scoresheet + a scrubbable event log fused into the controller — no legacy box has an event history, they delegate it to a human with paper. (2) The celebrate row with an All/Board/Ribbon send-target collapses the Daktronics All Sport (score) ↔ Show Control (presentation) seam that incumbents keep on two boxes with two operators. (3) The Hand-off QR is the multi-operator-without-multi-hardware move the RSR-paddle crews could never do.

The honest verdict: the chrome and the spatial grammar look like a nicer ScoreVision. The architecture (one brain owning score + screens + event log + hand-off) is already ahead of everyone. The RISK is that the FLAGSHIP "Full" view shows the operator the ScoreVision-looking part, not the part that's actually differentiated — so at a glance it reads "competent clone" rather than "category leap." You're losing the demo in the first five seconds even though the bones are superior. The fix is not to abandon the symmetric panels (operators have 40 years of muscle memory there) but to demote them to the "correct/detail" mode and lead with the verb-grid + armed-cue + auto-pilot spine that none of the four research clusters' products combine.

## Is "controls on each team's side" the most functional model?

"Controls on each team's side" is the RIGHT default for the single highest-frequency action (score +1) and the WRONG sole organizing principle for the whole console.

WHY IT'S RIGHT FOR SCORING (keep it): Object-first / direct-manipulation maps the operator's eyes 1:1 to the pool — Home-left, Away-right, no translation step (research cluster 5, cluster 1 'spatial muscle memory'). For the 99%-frequency +1, direct increment with zero confirm beats event-first: event-first (press GOAL → then pick team) ADDS a mode and a tap, which the research explicitly calls 'slower and more error-prone than direct manipulation for the high-frequency score case.' vMix proves the optimum — each key encodes team+delta so there is no mode at all. So keep per-team panels for score/shots/timeouts.

WHY IT'S NOT THE WHOLE ANSWER (three documented failures):

1. SYMMETRIC-MIRROR MODE ERROR. Visually identical Home/Away panels make operators commit to the wrong side — 'instant and humiliating on a public scoreboard' (cluster 5); a textbook mode-error trap (Norman/Buxton). Your mockup is literally 'TWO SYMMETRIC team panels' — symmetric is the danger word. Fix is modeless-by-identity (full-bleed team color, logo, jersey-tinted +1, color-flash on commit), not switching to event-first.

2. TAPS-PER-EVENT FOR COMPOUND PLAYS. A water-polo goal often implies assist + shot-clock reset + restart + possible exclusion clear. Pure per-side panels make each a separate hunt-and-tap. DakStats 'Suggested Plays' / FIBA 'Predictive Workflows' collapse a multi-part play to ~2 taps by auto-arming the next likely action — per-team panels alone give none of that.

3. EYES-UP OPERATION. The strongest live surfaces (Stream Deck/Companion, Ableton grids, ETC Eos, foot pedals) are event-first VERB GRIDS fired by reflex with state reported back by color — hands and eyes stay on the game. A read-and-adjust two-panel FORM forces the operator to look down and parse; wrong default for a phone-at-the-pool operator.

MOST FUNCTIONAL MODEL (synthesis): a THREE-TIER hybrid, not 'controls on each side.' Tier-1 high-frequency reversible (score ±, shots) = per-team panels, direct increment, zero confirm, modeless-by-identity. Tier-2 compound/event plays = predictive next-action chips that auto-arm after the primary tap (~2 taps). Tier-3 public/irreversible (celebration, horn, reset, end-period) = armed-cue LOAD→GO with a NEXT-UP preview + guarded confirm + cooldown (ETC Eos + QLab + aviation guarded switch). So per-team-side is a correct LAYER, not the architecture — the console should be a verb-grid-with-armed-cues spine that USES per-team panels for the scoring layer, the inverse of leading with the panels.

## Recommended flagship paradigm

Ship an EVENT-SOURCED, AUTO-ARMED VERB CONSOLE — "the show calls itself, you confirm" — built on three layers, with the per-team panels demoted from the headline to a detail mode.

The single bold-but-shippable direction: make the flagship "Full" view a verb-grid command surface fed by a forward-looking Run-of-Show spine, where the live game state auto-ARMS the next beat (never auto-fires) and the operator commits with one glance + one tap. Concretely:

1. SPINE (the headline): promote the bottom Run-of-Show rail from a backward undo log into a bidirectional event-sourced spine with hard NOW (red) / NEXT (yellow) states borrowed from Viz Mosart. It is simultaneously the official scoresheet, the multi-level cross-surface undo (every chip is a Command object; any board/ribbon/overlay is a projection), and the thing the auto-pilot pre-stages. This one rail is your entire categorical advantage made visible.

2. AUTO-ARM CO-PILOT (the demo): because one brain owns score + clock + exclusions + screens, the game state drives what lands in NEXT. Goal delta → 'Celebrate #7' (bound to roster identity) arms itself; clock to 0:00 in Q4 → 'Final deck' arms; exclusion entered → man-up scene arms and the auto-calculated re-entry countdown + absolute re-entry game-time posts to the ribbon (the digital Exclusion Secretary). The operator confirms with a tap or a $20 BLE foot pedal. Nobody — not Daktronics' two-box split, not Pixellot's operator-removed automation — offers auto-pilot AND bespoke human show-calling on the same board. This is the wedge; make it the first 30 seconds of the demo.

3. THREE-TIER INTERACTION GRAMMAR matched to reversibility (the safety story): Tier-1 high-frequency reversible (score ±, shots) = direct-increment, zero confirm, on modeless-by-identity team panels (full-bleed color, jersey-tinted +1, color-flash on commit, auto white-left/dark-right) — this is where the per-team-side layout LIVES, as a layer not the architecture. Tier-2 compound plays = predictive next-action chips (DakStats/FIBA) collapsing goal→assist→clear to ~2 taps. Tier-3 public/irreversible (celebration, horn, reset, end-period) = ETC-Eos LOAD→GO armed cue + QLab double-GO cooldown + guarded confirm. Every committed action self-labels with team+cap so the operator never holds "which side" in working memory; <100ms optimistic local paint with a local→server→on-board delivery dot so they can SEE it reached the LED.

Why this is the right bet and still shippable: it preserves the 40-year muscle memory (panels are still there, side still pinned) so a veteran timer trusts it on sight, while leading with the auto-armed spine that no incumbent's architecture can replicate. It directly serves the water-polo beta (shot-clock slaving, exclusion re-entry math, sprint cap) and it scales the same design system across all ~13 views because everything is a projection of one event log and every surface is a scoped view of one game state. The boldness is the auto-arm-never-auto-fire co-pilot; the shippability is that every piece (undo rail, QR hand-off, send-target selector, ⌨ set-time) already exists in the mockup — you're re-architecting the relationship between them, not building net-new primitives. Lead with the spine and the co-pilot in the demo; keep the symmetric panels as the comfortable, trusted detail mode underneath.

## Ranked "do-what-no-one-does" differentiators

### 1. Auto-calculated exclusion re-entry time (digital Exclusion Secretary)  _(difficulty: medium)_
- **What:** On any exclusion: one tap on the team panel → cap-number chip → auto-spawn a running 20s box-clock chip that shows BOTH the live countdown AND the absolute re-entry game-time (e.g. out at 2:13 → re-entry 1:53), color-flashes at 0, auto-clears on goal, auto-starts/pauses with the game clock, rolls up expired exclusions, and fires a 'player may re-enter' flash to the ribbon. Replaces the human flag-raiser entirely.
- **Why no one does it:** Legacy consoles can't compute or display it on a 16-char 2-line LCD, so the entire NFHS desk delegates it to a dedicated 'Exclusion Secretary' doing arithmetic by hand and raising colored flags. Broadcast/stat tools are sport-agnostic and have no native exclusion concept. Nobody owns BOTH the clock state AND a screen big enough to show the math.
- **Operator payoff:** Eliminates an entire human role and the most error-prone, anxiety-inducing mental arithmetic at the table. This is the single most water-polo-specific, demo-able 'how did I live without this' win — and it directly serves the beta sport.

### 2. Score-feed auto-pilot with one-glance override (auto-arm, never auto-fire)  _(difficulty: high)_
- **What:** Ingest the live game state (you already own it, or parse a CTS/Daktronics tap-in) so the console PRE-ARMS the next presentation beat instead of firing it: clock hits 0:00 in Q4 → 'Final / celebration deck' goes to NEXT (yellow); a goal delta pre-arms 'Celebrate #7' bound to that cap number's name/photo; an exclusion arms the man-up scene. A persistent 'NEXT UP' strip shows what's armed; the operator confirms with one tap (or foot pedal). Auto-suggests, the human commits.
- **Why no one does it:** Daktronics splits score (All Sport) and show (Show Control) across two boxes/people with zero shared state, so neither can pre-arm the other. Pixellot/NFHS achieve full automation only by REMOVING the operator and bespoke show-calling. No one offers full automation AND human show-calling on the same board because no one owns score + screens + rundown in one brain. This is the core VenueOS thesis made visible.
- **Operator payoff:** Collapses the 4-person NFHS desk to one calm person who mostly confirms. Turns the scary 'auto-celebration' (that operators normally disable) into a trusted co-pilot. This is the flagship demo moment.

### 3. Run-of-Show as event-sourced, reversible-across-every-surface command log  _(difficulty: medium)_
- **What:** Promote the bottom undo rail from a backward log to the canonical event database: every committed action is a Command object (execute/unexecute) rendered as a named chip ('GOAL Dodgers #7'). Any board, ribbon, scorebug overlay, and stat feed is a pure PROJECTION of the log, so one tap on any chip undoes it live across ALL surfaces at once, per-period totals auto-foot, and you export the official scoresheet PDF at game end. Each chip carries a 3-state delivery dot (local → server → on-board confirmed).
- **Why no one does it:** Legacy boxes have only -1 and 'Recall Shot Time'; the official record is literally a parallel paper scoresheet a human cross-foots every period. Stat apps have an editable play-by-play but don't drive the live board. Nobody makes the live board, the official record, AND a bulletproof multi-surface undo the SAME object.
- **Operator payoff:** Console becomes controller + official record + 'Cmd-Z for the real world' in one. Recovery from any mistake is one tap and the operator can SEE it reached the LED, not just the database. Kills the parallel-paper ritual.

### 4. Modeless-by-identity team panels (kills the symmetric-mirror error by construction)  _(difficulty: low)_
- **What:** Make each team panel carry overwhelming, always-on identity: full-bleed team color as panel background, logo watermark, jersey-color-tinted +1 button, and a brief color-flash of THAT panel on every commit. Auto-map white-left/dark-right from each team's brand kit to match the board. A one-tap 'Swap sides' mirrors the legacy double-sided insert.
- **Why no one does it:** Consumer scorekeepers ship visually identical mirror panels and under-invest here (research calls it a 'textbook mode-error trap'). Legacy consoles solve side-mapping with a manual chore — relabeling dark/white and physically flipping a double-sided plastic insert.
- **Operator payoff:** The wrong-side score error — instant and humiliating on a public board — becomes structurally impossible because the side announces itself. Removes the manual side-mapping chore. Cheap to build, disproportionately trust-building.

### 5. Non-destructive 'Set exact' on every numeric field (eliminates the legacy modal foot-gun)  _(difficulty: low)_
- **What:** Generalize the existing ⌨ Set-time type-in to every numeric field: long-press the number (or tap a small ✎) opens a numeric pad with LIVE PREVIEW and an explicit Cancel that REVERTS. No 'mode key first', no zero-fill-on-early-confirm, no 'CLEAR twice to escape'.
- **Why no one does it:** Every legacy console's modal EDIT/SET grammar is the documented source of most operator errors — Daktronics needs CLEAR pressed twice to abort, CTS mis-parses time without tenths, Nevco silently zero-fills. Operator manuals spend pages on the 'oops button'.
- **Operator payoff:** An entire class of canonical errors simply cannot occur. A volunteer can correct an exact value without fear. Low cost, high safety, and it's the antidote to the incumbents' most-complained-about behavior.

### 6. Verb-grid 'Run' surface with state-feedback keys + armed-cue for irreversibles  _(difficulty: high)_
- **What:** Make the DEFAULT live surface a Companion-style fixed grid of large, color-feedback verb keys (HOME +1 / AWAY +1 / SHOT 30 / EXCL HOME / TIMEOUT / HORN / GOAL CEL / POWER PLAY), each firing a stacked multi-action (GOAL = score+1 → horn → ribbon cel → log). Keys report live state by color (EXCL key shows live :20 + red; HORN greys during cooldown). Public/irreversible cues use ETC-Eos LOAD→GO + QLab double-GO cooldown. The symmetric team panels become the 'detail/correct' tab.
- **Why no one does it:** Sports consoles are object-first field editors with no verb grid, no armed cue, no preview/program. Broadcast tools have the armed-cue discipline but no native sport scoring and a brutal learning curve. Stream Deck/Companion has the grid but needs a $200 box and power-user programming. Nobody ships the grid PRE-BUILT per sport on a phone.
- **Operator payoff:** Eyes-up, reflex-fire control; status read preattentively by color; accidental double-horn/celebration killed by cooldown + arm. This is what makes the console feel like a pro control surface instead of a form.

### 7. Multi-operator crew from QR hand-off — role-scoped phones binding to one game state  _(difficulty: medium)_
- **What:** The Hand-off QR spins up role-scoped views (clock-only 'paddle', PA, show-caller, penalty box) on any phone, all bound to ONE shared live game state, with the Run-of-Show synced across all of them so a head operator can undo a junior's mistake from their own device. Optional Apple-Watch/second-device satellite for HORN / ALL-CLEAR / ARM-NEXT.
- **Why no one does it:** Legacy crews split duty only via dedicated hardware (RSR paddle, separate shot-clock head) with NO shared state. ScoreVision requires pre-provisioned second iPads + training. Nobody makes the second seat a 5-second scan against one event-sourced truth.
- **Operator payoff:** Scales from one calm solo operator to a 4-person crew with zero extra hardware — and the crew can self-correct because they all see the same reversible log. Matches the real NFHS desk choreography without the boxes.

### 8. Zero-config game night: cloud profiles + pre-game manufacturer-quirk checklist  _(difficulty: low)_
- **What:** Pre-stage rosters, cap numbers, period/OT lengths, shot-clock rules, and sponsor rotation in the cloud before arrival (Nevco/CTS profiles, but shareable and handed to a volunteer via the QR). Pair with a software pre-flight checklist card drawn from the NFHS timer guide (sides correct? shot clock blanks <30s? horn tones? timeout-cancel behavior?).
- **Why no one does it:** Legacy profiles are local-only and per-box; cross-vendor quirks are tribal knowledge NFHS literally tells timers to re-check every game. Nobody encodes the manufacturer-difference checklist into software.
- **Operator payoff:** Game night is 'press Start.' A first-time volunteer is as safe as a 20-year veteran. Removes hostile live setup (channel/PAN matching, side relabeling).

### 9. Optional stats-on-the-side (dismissible follow-up chips) fused with live board control  _(difficulty: medium)_
- **What:** Steal GameChanger/ScoreVision: after a credited goal, an OPTIONAL dismissible 'who assisted? (cap #)' chip enriches the box score / shot chart from the SAME taps that drive the board — never blocking the next action. The accolade button is pre-bound to the scorer's identity.
- **Why no one does it:** Stat apps do beautiful follow-up prompts but don't drive a live board or clock; board consoles drive the board but capture no stats. The two have never been the same product.
- **Operator payoff:** Free box score + shot charts as a byproduct of running the game, with zero added friction for the operator who just wants to run the board. Sellable as analytics on top of presentation.

## Risks / watch-outs

- Auto-pilot trust cliff: a wrong prediction at a high-stakes moment (arming GOAL Home when it was Away) erodes operator trust fast, and once burned they disable auto features (the exact reason operators turn off existing auto-celebration). Mitigate: auto-ARM only, never auto-fire; make the armed NEXT beat glanceably obvious; one-tap dismiss; and gate it behind a confidence threshold from the score feed.
- Demoting the symmetric panels could spook the veteran timer who trusts the 40-year layout. Mitigate: keep panels one tap away as the 'detail/correct' mode, keep Home-left/Away-right pinning sacred, and never reflow responsively in a way that swaps sides.
- Reliability bar: the incumbents' membrane-key-over-serial boxes have no app to crash and no Wi-Fi to drop mid-period. A phone-driven console must clear or visibly fall back. Mitigate: local-first optimistic state + offline queue, a per-surface health pill (board/ribbon/stream), local→server→on-board delivery dots, and an optional BLE foot pedal so the most-pressed control (clock) survives a flaky link.
- Confirmation habituation: if Tier-3 guards fire too often, operators blind-tap through them (NN/G 'cry wolf'). Mitigate: reserve hold-to-confirm strictly for genuinely irreversible/public actions; everything reversible gets instant undo instead, never a dialog.
- Cross-browser/Chromium-83 + WebKit exposure: the live board/ribbon surfaces ship to NovaStar Taurus (Chromium 83) and the console runs on Safari/iPad — no inset shorthand or gap in player/widget paths, WebKit-test the console interactivity before 'done.' A symmetric color-flash/animation that works in Chrome can no-op on the Taurus.
- Scope creep across 13 views: the auto-arm spine, predictive chips, verb grid, and multi-operator hand-off are each substantial. Mitigate: ship the event-sourced Run-of-Show spine + modeless-by-identity panels + non-destructive Set-exact first (all low/medium, high payoff), then layer the auto-arm co-pilot and verb grid.
- Predictive/keyboard-shorthand features assume context (keyboard, literate operator) absent on a wet phone at a loud pool deck — they must degrade to big tappable verb tiles, never be the only path.

## The three concept mockups (in `scratch/design/console-concepts/`)

- **Event Deck** — Verb-first event stream — the live event timeline IS the interface. Press a VERB, pick who, done. Two taps, never "hunt the right panel."
  - Kill the symmetric two-team mirror: one big verb-entry surface + a living, fully-reversible Run-of-Show spine that is simultaneously the controller, the official scoresheet, and Cmd+Z for the real world.
- **Command Console (eyes-up)** — Eyes-up verb console: giant labeled cue keys + a "/" command palette (type "g7", "ex 11", "to home") + a big glanceable board mirror — every control shows its hotkey inline, so the operator drives the game watching the pool, not the screen. Tier-3 irreversibles use ETC-Eos LOAD→TAKE arming + horn double-tap cooldown; the Run-of-Show rail is the event-sourced, undo-anywhere spine with NOW/NEXT auto-arm.
  - Drive scoreboard + LED ribbon + overlay with your eyes on the game: one row of huge hotkey'd cue keys, a Cmd-K command palette ("g7" = goal #7), and a broadcast board mirror that confirms state at a glance.
- **Show-Caller Rundown** — Broadcast rundown — armed segments you ARM then TAKE, with a NOW/NEXT row, a hard TAKE button, per-item surface targets (Board/Ribbon/Stream), and live multi-operator presence (scorekeeper · show-caller · PA) over one shared event-sourced game state.
  - Brings the broadcast truck to the gym: a vertical show rundown with NOW-on-program / NEXT-armed rows and one big TAKE, run by a multi-operator room — the differentiator no HS/college venue product ships.
